# V1.6.2 reconnaissance — government & venue sources

**Scope:** read-only public-GET reconnaissance of the government and venue sources on the user's Suzhou source list.
**Probe date:** 2026-10-13 (Asia/Shanghai), evening. **"Future" below means on or after 2026-10-13** (an event starting today is still bookable).
**Method:** Python 3.14.5 (`backend\.venv`), httpx 0.28.1 + BeautifulSoup. TLS verification was **always on** — no `verify=False` anywhere. Public GET only; no logins, no tokens, no protected APIs, no JS execution. User-Agent: `MendunerEventResearch/1.5 (academic prototype; limited public event pages)`.
No file outside this report was created or modified in the repo. All probe scripts were written to `%TEMP%\menduner_recon\` and deleted on completion; the raw response bodies kept during probing were part of that deleted scratch directory, so only the observations transcribed below remain.

**Reading the "real dated future events" column:** the count is the number of explicitly dated, *future* items visible in the served HTML after decoding, with tags stripped. **Events starting today (2026-10-13) count as future** (still bookable), which is why a 2026-10-13 - 2026-11-01 exhibition is counted once. Publication timestamps, template/placeholder years, and year-less `M月D日` strings that could not be tied to a year are **not** counted — they are called out in the notes instead.

The listing markup is trivially parseable and needs no JS:

```html
<a href="festival-detail.aspx?id=2026101009515301">苏州保利大剧院：《音你而来》A-minor 人声乐团专场音乐会</a>
<div class="date"><div>活动时间：2026-11-07</div></div>
```

Dates appear as a single date (`活动时间：2026-10-18`) or a range (`活动时间：2026-12-04 - 2026-12-05`), always `YYYY-MM-DD`.

Detail pages are fully server-rendered and carry separable, quotable fields — verified verbatim on the 保利 page:

| Field | Observed value |
| --- | --- |
| 来源 | 苏州保利大剧院微信号 |
| 时间 | 2026-11-07 |
| 地点 | 苏州市吴中区东苑路1号苏州保利大剧院大剧场 |
| 演出时间 | 2026年11月7日19:30 |
| 演出地点 | 苏州市吴中区东苑路1号苏州保利大剧院大剧场 |
| 购票方式 | `原信息链接：https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/87jCpIw8Ydk3LooRDLqD3A` |

Two caveats an implementer must know:

1. **Invalid IDs fail soft.** `festival-detail.aspx?id=9999999999999999` returns **HTTP 200** with a 196-char boilerplate page and no fields. Detail acceptance must require a real 时间/地点, never a 200 status.
2. **The calendar is not ordered by event date.** Pages 1–12 yield 12/12/12/6/2/8/9/12/9/4/6/4 future items — i.e. recent-past and future entries are interleaved, so the crawler must page through and filter rather than stop at the first past-dated page. Depth is large (the pager advertises 662 pages), so bounded paging plus a future-date filter is required.
3. **The portal itself is the mirror.** Its 来源 field names the WeChat account (`…微信号`) and links the original `mp.weixin.qq.com` post. This is the only sanctioned, public, web-accessible representation of 公众号 content found in this recon — it does **not** make the WeChat accounts themselves crawlable.

## 4. Cross-cutting findings

1. **`robots.txt` is the systemic blocker for four of the government hosts — and *not* a blocker for the venue hosts.** Four hosts return **HTTP 200 with the site's own homepage as the robots body** (`www.sipac.gov.cn`, `www.suzhou.gov.cn`, `wglj.suzhou.gov.cn`, `tiyuju.suzhou.gov.cn`; plus `www.hanshansi.org`), and `www.szmuseum.com` 302-redirects `/robots.txt` to `/404.html`. `crawler/http.py` `_policy()` treats a 200 HTML body as "no usable robots rules" and then **fails closed** (`RuntimeError`), so **those sources are currently uncollectable regardless of how good their event data is.** This is the highest-leverage fix available, but it is a policy decision (accept a documented "non-rules 200 ⇒ permissive" rule) — **I did not change any code.**
   *Contrast:* `www.suzhouexpo.com`, `www.szosc.cn`, `jinjilake.sipac.gov.cn`, `visitsz.wglj.suzhou.gov.cn`, `www.rd.suzhou.gov.cn` and `www.hanshansi.cn` all return a clean **404** (no rules ⇒ permissive), and `www.sscac.com.cn` returns a 0-byte 200. So **the three venue sites that turned out to be event-empty are the ones that were never blocked** — their emptiness is a real content finding, not a permissions artefact.
2. **Three supplied URLs are wrong, one of them a different city.** `www.suzhoupcc.com` (arts centre) is NXDOMAIN; the real site is `www.sscac.com.cn`. `szrd.gov.cn`/`szzx.gov.cn` are **Shenzhen** (深圳人大网/政协深圳市委员会), not Suzhou. `tyj.suzhou.gov.cn` (sports bureau) is NXDOMAIN; the real host is `tiyuju.suzhou.gov.cn`.
3. **The Shenzhen/Suzhou trap is worth flagging explicitly**, because `sz*` looks like Suzhou and the two Shenzhen sites are HTTP-reachable and date-rich (34 KB and 53 KB with 43 and 3 full dates). A crawler configured with those hosts would silently ingest the wrong city.
4. **Three "authoritative" venue sites are JS shells that serve empty data:** `sscac.com.cn` (Nuxt, `state:{list:{list:[]}}`), `pcc.sipac.gov.cn` (418 KB shell, 665 visible chars), and the `news.qq.com` 苏州工业园区发布 mirror (3 visible chars). A status-200 check would call all three "working".
5. **`szosc.cn` is the clearest false positive in the whole set.** The registry records it as **SUCCESS** and it does look like a live ticketing page ("在线购票 >"), but every one of its 135 dates is in the **past** and the list has no pagination. It must not be treated as a live event feed.
6. **Two independent TLS-level problems on this machine:** `szrd.gov.cn`/`szzx.gov.cn` fail with `BAD_ECPOINT` and `www.zx.suzhou.gov.cn` with `SSLV3_ALERT_HANDSHAKE_FAILURE`, regardless of certificate trust. No verification was disabled to work around them.
7. **`szmuseum.com` needs two small HTTP-client accommodations, not a policy exception.** Its `Cache Control:` header violates RFC 9110 and httpx refuses the response before any parsing; and its `robots.txt` 302-redirect is rejected by the existing same-path redirect rule. A tolerant transport plus a documented robots-redirect rule would unlock a fully server-rendered, static museum site.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Add a `visitsz.wglj.suzhou.com.cn` adapter first.** It is the only host in this recon that clears every gate: permissive robots (404), valid TLS, pure server-rendered HTML, a stable `festival-activity.aspx?page=N` list, a stable `festival-detail.aspx?id=<16-digit>` detail pattern, and **92 real future events on 12 pages** covering 苏州文化艺术中心, 保利大剧院, 苏州湾大剧院, 苏州美术馆, 苏州奥体 and more. Enforce the caveats in §2: require a real 时间/地点 (never trust HTTP 200 alone) and page-and-filter rather than stopping at the first past-dated page.
2. **The four government hosts (`sipac`, `suzhou.gov.cn`, `wglj`, `tiyuju`) are structurally fine but event-poor — and currently fail-closed on robots.** All are static, valid-TLS and date-rich, but every date confirmed so far is a publication timestamp, and all four fail the current robots rule. Before investing in adapters, decide the documented policy for "non-rules robots 200" (finding 1) and resolve the year-less `M月D日` question (the 文广旅局 `yexiq` section and the 体育局 competition notices are the two places worth a second look).
3. **Do not build adapters for**: `suzhouexpo.com` (0 dates on 8 pages, token-dependent), `szosc.cn` (past-only archive), `sscac.com.cn`/`pcc.sipac.gov.cn` (empty SPA payloads), 寒山寺 (403), 华谊兄弟 (no usable TLS host), and the WeChat account (no official public mirror). Note that for expo/szosc/sscac the *robots* situation is clean — these are content verdicts, not access verdicts.
4. **Treat 华谊兄弟, 寒山寺, 金鸡湖美术馆 and 苏州国际博览中心 as documented coverage gaps.** The visitsz portal carries nothing for them, so no public static substitute currently exists.

**Surprises worth a second look by the Lead**

- The **best venue-event source is neither a venue nor a government body's own feed**, but the 文广旅局 tourism portal — and it already contains the WeChat posts that the source list could not reach.
- **`szosc.cn` is reported SUCCESS in the registry while serving an exclusively past-events archive** with no pagination.
- **Three of the requested sites do not exist as specified**, and two supplied hostnames belong to a different city (Shenzhen).
- **`szmuseum.com` is blocked by its own malformed HTTP header** (and a robots redirect), not by any access control.
- **`robots.txt` 200-but-HTML is the common case for the government hosts**, so the crawler's fail-closed rule — not the sites' content — is the binding constraint on the government half of this list. Conversely the three event-empty venue sites (`expo`, `szosc`, `sscac`) all pass robots cleanly, so their failure is genuine content absence.
